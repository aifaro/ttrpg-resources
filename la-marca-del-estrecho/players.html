Synopsis (English)

Ceuta, September 1921. A man wrote to you four times. You never answered him. The fourth letter put you on a steamer.

Spain is bleeding out in Africa. In July, at a dry patch of nothing called Annual, twenty thousand men strung out by a general with more habit than plan came apart in three days. What followed was not a retreat but a rout — thousands of men throwing away their rifles to run lighter. Nobody has finished counting the dead. In the cafés of Madrid they are demanding an explanation that nobody can give.

Ceuta is the funnel that war pours through. Fifty thousand inhabitants, fourteen thousand evacuees from Melilla sleeping in the warehouses, hospital beds out in the corridors, and a queue of women at the door of the Comandancia every morning asking after names. Ships come in with the wounded and go out with conscripts. The city is white and packed tight on its isthmus, held to Africa by a thread of land and to Spain by fourteen miles of sea: Ceuta has been Spanish for centuries, and that is why there is a garrison, a Comandancia General and Spanish paperwork on this side of the water. And the city has decided, from top to bottom and without anyone giving the order, not to look straight at what has just happened.

You are not going to Ceuta for the war. You are going because Paco Guerrero wrote to you: an acquaintance who treats you like his closest friend, and to whom you owe a glass of wine. A veteran reporter for the Telegrama del Rif — fifty years old, ink-stained waistcoat, one of those men who call you my dear fellow after meeting you briefly years ago. He wrote to you as one of his own. You owed him a glass of wine.

Four letters, June to September.

19 June. «One of those stories of yours has landed in my lap. You know the kind: the strange ones. Why don’t you come over for a few days? The first glass is on me.»

24 July. «I am writing fast and with my stomach turned over. You will have read about Annual. And even so — forgive me, I am ashamed of it — that is not what I want to talk to you about. I have pulled at the thread, and the thread does not end.»

21 August. «You know I am not a man who frightens easily. I have seen the trenches, I have seen typhus, I have seen politicians up close. That is why this is so hard to write. I write to you by day. At night I no longer trust what I write.»

1 September. «No time for a proper letter. Come. Come now. Take the first steamer.»

And then you cross the Strait. Each of you on your own, with what you are wearing, on a steamer full of reinforcements. Half an hour of coal smoke against the levanter and Africa begins to show through the haze: the Monte Hacho first, a hump of brown rock with the profile of a man lying on his back, and beneath it the city, white and packed tight.

You bring an address, four letters each less sane than the one before, a strange drawing in the last of them, and the uncomfortable feeling of having taken three months to do something that cost nothing. What you do not bring is the faintest idea of what you have put your foot into.

Because something has been happening in Ceuta for months, and it did not begin with you. It is an entire city so full of a real horror that it has no room left inside to notice there is another one.

You are not the first to look at this. You are the first who came to look on purpose.

What kind of game this is. Cosmic horror with dirty hands. This is Pulp Cthulhu, so your investigators are capable people: they take more punishment, they hit more often and they have special talents, and the adventure is built for you to fight, run across rooftops, negotiate with men of rank and climb a mountain at night with whatever you have managed to put together. Nobody goes mad from looking at an odd drawing. Madness comes more slowly, and for better reasons.

And it is real historical horror, not scenery. Everything underneath it — Annual, the evacuees, the censorship, the paperwork of an overloaded garrison town, the charge of the Alcántara — happened. The real part alone is already terrible.

System: Pulp Cthulhu (7th ed.) · Investigators: three to five · Length: around ten sessions · Where and when: Ceuta, September 1921.

Before we start. We run a session zero, and this adventure genuinely needs one. The material is hard and deliberately present: a military disaster very recent for your characters, murdered prisoners whose bodies have still not been recovered, a father’s grief for his son, madness and psychiatric confinement, and the violence of a war upon civilians. We will agree on lines and veils together. Bring along, if you like, an idea of who your character is and of why Paco Guerrero had their name written down in an address book.
