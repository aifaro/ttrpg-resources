Synopsis

Ceuta, September 1921

Spain is bleeding out in Africa. The Disaster of Annual — ten thousand dead in a matter of days, entire garrisons wiped off the map, officers slaughtered in their own tents — has left a humiliated army and a country demanding answers that no one can provide. In Ceuta, the gateway to the Spanish Protectorate of Morocco, life continues by inertia: wounded soldiers arriving on steamships, spies trading secrets in cafés, refugees whispering rumors that no one dares confirm. Every shadow moving in the distance awakens a suspicion that can no longer be distinguished from fear.

But there are things worse than war.

Something stirs in Ceuta. Something that appears in no military report, no diplomatic dispatch. Incidents that the authorities rush to cover up. Soldiers who go mad for no reason. Merchants found dead with expressions no forensic examiner can explain. And a color — a yellow that belongs to no known catalog — appearing in places where reality itself is defied.

The investigators arrive in Ceuta for reasons of their own, and find themselves, from the very first moment, at the center of something they do not understand. To unravel it, they must move between two worlds: that of high society — offices, cafés, and literary gatherings — and that of the medina's alleyways, where languages are spoken that appear in no dictionary. They will forge unlikely alliances with people who, under any other circumstances, would never have exchanged a word. They will discover that the line between victim and executioner is thinner than they believed. And they will learn that there are doors the Ancient Ones sealed for reasons humanity has long forgotten — doors that wait only to be opened again.

Time is running out. September presses on. And above the fortress city of Ceuta — where legend holds that Hercules raised one of his mythic columns — the sky seems thinner than it should.

The Yellow Sign is an adventure for Pulp Cthulhu set in Ceuta in 1921, blending investigation, action, and cosmic horror in a rich and rarely explored historical setting for the roleplaying game: the City of Four Cultures, the Key of the Strait, at one of the most critical moments of the Rif War. For 2–5 investigators. Estimated length: 4–6 sessions.
